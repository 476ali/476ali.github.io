title: 'Memory of a Loss'
private: true
---

Isn’t never having had a dearest something the same disposition as having a something?
The pain of loss or the profound passion of discovery is what only which has the cruel
power to pluck the strings of a heart. And so to be born an orphan is in a strange sense
luckier for they aren’t bound to be neither broken nor battered by the fading away of
something they’ll surely hold dear, something they’ll love, they’ll miss. In the
phenomenological aspect, it is the memory of a loss, not that fact of it, that puts a
nail in the beating.

To forget is benediction; to be reminded is torture. Yet this torture is no less
deserving of experience than its absence: this is a juncture of reflection where one
perhaps realizes the nature of life itself. To have loved is to have been hurt; to have
lived is to have had regrets; this we all in due time must realize: whilst this may be so
hurt and regret don’t entail the necessity for self-condemnation nor rectification, for
all that one knows the residue of hurt is not a scab but a callous skin and the residue
of regret is not a lack of contentment but a faint image of how the rays of life are
laid, growing ever in vigour and detail. They aren’t undesirable appendages of living to
be incised away but a constitutive part of it as how a pendulum that swings forth must
swing back.
